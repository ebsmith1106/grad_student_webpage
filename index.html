About

Research & Projects

Undergraduate research · 2026

MJO Lead–Lag Relationship with North Atlantic Tropical Storm Formation

Can today's Madden–Julian Oscillation phase hint at Atlantic storm formation weeks ahead? For 2009–2025, storms formed about twice as often when the MJO had been in phases 1–2 twelve days earlier as when it had been in phases 6–7.

Undergraduate honors thesis · University at Albany

Environmental and Structural Drivers of Hurricane Edouard (2014) Intensification

Combining P-3 tail Doppler radar, dropsondes, satellite imagery and lightning data to examine what helped Edouard strengthen, including a lightning outbreak ahead of intensification and a small vortex tilt, and what held it back: dry mid-level air on its east side.

Forecasting Tools

The websites and tools I use to make a forecast.

General forecasting

  • AguaceroWX

    A weather visualization website with models, satellite, MRMS, RTMA, NWS alerts, local storm reports (LSRs), and more, with nearly every feature customizable. This is my most-used forecasting website and where I start my top-down forecasting approach.

  • Tropical Tidbits

    An easy way to look at the latest model runs and see where a system is placed when following a forecast. Also a great tool for following tropical systems.

  • Storm Prediction Center

    I use this website for a range of reasons, most often for a quick look at the day's severe weather across the CONUS or for the detailed forecast discussion for my area.

  • NCAR Real-Time Weather

    I use this website for quick surface observations.

  • WPC Surface Maps

    Used mainly to look at frontal boundaries and precipitation discussions.

  • Dr. Nick Bassill’s NYS Mesonet Page

    For any state-based current or 24-hour maps of recent weather, I go straight to Nick’s Mesonet page.

  • Dr. Thomas Galarneau’s Real-Time QG Diagnostics

    If I really need to get into the weeds with synoptic forcing, this is the site I gravitate toward, and the one I was taught on.

  • Aurora Borealis Observations & Forecast

    Pretty, but also useful for forecasting from an emergency management perspective, since it can affect satellite radio navigation systems.

  • NY State Fall Foliage Report

    Maps the stage of fall foliage across the state, including where it has peaked. An important indicator for travel and tourism, and for the impacts of rain and snow.

  • Aviation Weather Center

    A great source for any aviation-related hazards.

  • Australian Government Bureau of Meteorology

    Used most often to view the current phase of the MJO when forecasting on subseasonal-to-seasonal (S2S) timescales.

  • College of DuPage

    A great interface for quickly viewing satellite imagery or mosaic radar.

  • Michael Fisher’s TC Atlas

    A fantastic website for real-time or archived tropical storm data. I have used this site in research and find it a great tool for any verification I need to do against my own plots.

  • NOAA Damage Assessment Toolkit

    I have used this when viewing tornado damage in NY. Accurate and detailed information.

  • NYSDEC Air Quality Forecast

    This is where the NWS gets its information for its air quality alerts, so it is better to go straight to the source.

  • CPC Outlook

    8–14 day temperature and precipitation outlooks, plus drought information.

  • USGS National Water Dashboard

    River flood stages and daily graphs. A great source for ice jams in the Northeast.
